# Evidence and limitations

The sources below inform component techniques, not the effectiveness of StudyFlow as a whole. Starting rules, restart plans, domain-specific performance checks and optional progress notes are workflow designs, not independently validated interventions. Adapt intervals, durations and item counts to the task and observed performance.

1. Dunlosky et al. (2013), Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques. Reviews practice testing, distributed practice, self-explanation and interleaving, including limitations. It does not justify stacking every method or prescribing a universal schedule.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/
2. Karpicke & Blunt (2011), Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning than Elaborative Studying with Concept Mapping. Supports retrieval-based learning and inference checks in the science-text tasks studied, not exclusive closed-book practice for every task and stage.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/
3. NSW Centre for Education Statistics and Evaluation (2018), Cognitive load theory in practice. A practice guide supporting adjustment to prior knowledge, worked examples, increasing independence and reducing irrelevant information. It does not establish one compulsory teaching sequence.
   https://education.nsw.gov.au/about-us/education-data-and-research/cese/publications/practical-guides-for-educators/cognitive-load-theory-in-practice
4. Bastani et al. (2025), Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. A field experiment motivating separate evaluation of assisted answers and unassisted performance. Tutor guardrails mitigated harm in that study; it does not show that all AI tutoring helps or harms. The paper has an author-affiliation correction.
   https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
5. Koriat & Bjork (2006), Illusions of competence during study can be remedied by manipulations that enhance learners' sensitivity to retrieval conditions at test. Studies the mismatch between answers visible during study and unavailable during testing. Testing experience and delayed judgments reduced bias under the studied conditions. This informs the distinction between familiarity and retrieval, not a diagnosis based on one failed attempt.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17128596/
6. Pashler et al., Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence. Reviews evidence requirements for matching teaching to learning-style labels. This supports using task demands and observed performance rather than fixed labels, without denying preferences, ability differences or accessibility needs.
   https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/07/Pashler_McDaniel_Rohrer_Bjork_2009_PSPI.pdf
7. Richland, Kornell & Kao (2009), The pretesting effect: Do unsuccessful retrieval attempts enhance learning? Pretests helped subsequent learning under the studied conditions with relevant later learning or feedback. This is not encouragement to guess without correction; respect a request to skip pretesting and do not judge ability from a prelearning failure.
   https://learninglab.uchicago.edu/Publications_files/RichlandKornellKao.pdf
8. Chi et al. (1989), Self-Explanations: How Students Study and Use Examples in Learning to Solve Problems. Examines self-explanation, monitoring and later problem solving in example-based learning. This informs checking the conditions and reasons behind key steps, not requiring novices to derive unfamiliar material unaided.
   https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1
9. Brunmair & Richter (2019), Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. Effects depend on material and category relationships. This informs discriminating similar methods, not randomly mixing all subjects or assuming interleaving always beats blocked practice.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/
10. Vaughn, Dunlosky & Rawson (2016), Effects of successive relearning on recall: Does relearning override the effects of initial learning criterion? Studies relearning across sessions and initial criteria with word pairs. It informs combining retrieval, feedback and spaced relearning; its intervals and counts do not automatically transfer to long verbatim passages.
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27027887/

## How research changes decisions

| Component | Design implication | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval and self-testing | Check actual recall, including whether cues were used | Choose recall or recognition to match the outcome |
| Spacing | Distribute practice and adjust to performance | No universally optimal fixed interval |
| Fading help | Move between examples and independent practice as needed | Novices need not discover everything alone |
| Individual adjustment | Use prerequisites, task demands and attempts | Preferences are not proof of improved learning |
| Prequestions | Try, learn and correct | Failure before instruction is not an ability judgment |
| Self-explanation | Check a key step's reason and conditions | Supply missing prerequisites |
| Interleaving | Practise choosing among similar methods | Do not force mixing before basic competence |
| Delayed checks | Reduce reliance on answer-visible familiarity | Confidence ratings are optional; calibration is not guaranteed |
| Successive relearning | Reach a session criterion through retrieval and feedback, then revisit later | Session success is not durable retention |

Separate criteria for verbatim recall, paraphrase and application, along with stopping rules, are implementation choices. Psychology informs general learning; it does not turn this skill into clinical assessment or a psychology-course specialist.

Review-backlog rules combine task-load and time-management decisions. Prioritizing, pausing additions and editing cards have not been separately validated as this workflow.

The course cycle was adapted from a user-supplied learning routine. It combines explanation with application, requires a source or labeled inference for concept links, adjusts time and item counts, and permits later checks of material already understood. Component research does not validate this combination, a fixed 90-minute session or preparation exactly two days before class.

Bilingual responses, applied-skill standards and progress records extend task handling. They do not carry a claim of increased learning outcomes. Behavioral checks evaluate instruction following, not educational effectiveness.
